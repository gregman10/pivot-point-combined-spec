## Phase 1.1 — The $50M startup thought experiment, per available direction

Re-running per v1.2 against the Phase 0 industry-research directions, this time amplified to the combined-entity context.

### Direction 1 — Customer-Validation-First (KLAS as load-bearing claim)
*"If a $50M startup wanted to own this direction..."*
- They'd publish KLAS data continuously, not annually
- They'd compete on customer-told evidence
- They'd own the category claim "customer-graded HIT"

**Fits the combined entity?** PARTIAL. KLAS is real proof but only validates the execution side (Pivot Point's legacy). The ICG advisory side doesn't have a KLAS equivalent. Customer-validation-first amplifies HALF the distinction, not the whole.

### Direction 2 — Managed IT Services Category Leadership
*"If a $50M startup wanted to own this..."*
- They'd anchor to the specific KLAS category
- They'd compete on managed-services-as-the-relationship
- They'd own "Managed IT Performance" as the category

**Fits the combined entity?** PARTIAL. This was the right anchor for STANDALONE Pivot Point. For the combined entity, it under-claims — Managed IT is one of three combined offerings (Managed Services + Strategic Advisory + Talent Solutions). Anchoring only on Managed IT leaves ICG's advisory legacy on the table.

### Direction 3 — Senior-Only-Delivery (no pyramid leverage)
*"If a $50M startup wanted to own this..."*
- They'd guarantee senior consultants for the whole engagement
- They'd own "Senior Through Stabilization" or similar

**Fits the combined entity?** PARTIAL. Probably true at the combined entity, but not the strongest amplifier. ICG and Pivot Point each have senior depth, but neither has uniquely owned this slot.

### Direction 4 — AI-Augmented Healthcare IT
*"If a $50M startup wanted to own this..."*
- They'd use AI agents in consulting delivery
- They'd claim cost + consistency advantages

**Fits the combined entity?** NO. No public evidence either firm leads here. Would be aspirational.

### Direction 5 — Clinician-Adoption-First
*"If a $50M startup wanted to own this..."*
- They'd guarantee adoption metrics, not project completion
- They'd own "Clinician-Adopted HIT"

**Fits the combined entity?** PARTIAL. Adoption is a strong Pivot Point story but not a clean ICG story. Splits the integration narrative.

### Direction 6 — Specialty-Clinical-Setting Specialist
*"If a $50M startup wanted to own this..."*
- They'd specialize in one setting type (academic, pediatric, rural)

**Fits the combined entity?** NO. Combined entity covers across settings; specialty positioning would constrain.

### Direction 7 (NEW — derived from Phase A) — Strategy-Execution Integrity
*"If a $50M startup wanted to dominate HIT consulting in 5 years..."*
- They'd refuse to be either strategy-only OR execution-only
- They'd build a firm where strategy and execution sit under one accountability structure
- They'd claim the category that doesn't exist yet: HIT consulting WITHOUT the handoff between firms
- They'd back it with the only evidence anyone trusts: KLAS for execution + multi-year strategy advisory pedigree

**Fits the combined entity?** YES — PERFECTLY. This direction is exactly what the merger created. The PE investment thesis publicly stated this: "rapid structural change driven by workforce constraints, cost pressures, regulatory complexity and accelerating digital adoption" requires INTEGRATED platforms.

**New Way:** Unified Healthcare IT (one firm covering strategy through execution through talent, under one accountability structure)
