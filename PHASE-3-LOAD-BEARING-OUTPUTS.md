## [0] CATEGORY NARRATIVE

For two decades, hospital CIOs have hired one firm to design their HIT transformation and a different firm to run it. The advisory firm produces the strategy deck. The execution firm picks it up and tries to deliver it. The handoff between them is where the transformation goes to die.

That framing focuses on the wrong thing. CIOs aren't really shopping for a strategy firm and then an execution firm. They're shopping for a transformation that lands — clinically adopted, operationally stable, board-defensible. The split into two firms is a category accident, not a buyer requirement.

As healthcare IT has matured into a continuous discipline rather than a series of projects, the cost of the strategy-execution handoff has become structural. Boards see overruns and ask "who's accountable?" Each firm points at the other. The CIO is left holding both bills and the failure.

A new category is emerging from that gap. We call it Unified Healthcare IT. The defining shift is structural: one firm holds both the strategic design and the execution run, under one P&L, one accountability table, one continuous engagement.

The combined Pivot Point Consulting — Innovative Consulting Group's 22-year vendor-agnostic strategic advisory practice merged with Pivot Point's four-consecutive-year #1 KLAS Managed IT execution engine — is the proof case for that category. No other healthcare IT firm has both halves at this level of evidence.

## [1] CORE BELIEFS (three)

### Belief 1 — Where HIT transformations actually fail
**Most HIT transformations fail at the handoff between two firms — not at the strategy, not at the execution, but in the space between.**

Every CIO has lived this. Firm A delivers a polished strategy deck. Firm B picks up the deck and tries to execute. By month four, Firm A's senior partners are off the project. Firm B's senior leads are on three other accounts. The original outcomes get reinterpreted by people who weren't in the design conversations. The transformation drifts. The board asks why. Nobody owns the answer.

### Belief 2 — How boards think now
**The board no longer pays for strategy and execution as separate purchases. They pay for transformations that land.**

Hospital operating margins are squeezed. Boards are tired of paying twice — once for the strategy deck, once for the execution — and ending up with a transformation that didn't deliver. CFOs are pushing for outcome-tied engagements. The buying preference is shifting from "best strategy firm + best execution firm" to "one firm we can hold accountable for the whole thing."

### Belief 3 — Where the category is heading
**The next decade of healthcare IT belongs to firms that own both the design and the run, with proof on both sides. Half a firm cannot deliver a whole transformation.**

The PE thesis behind the Pivot Point + Innovative Consulting Group merger names this directly: healthcare needs integrated platforms, not point solutions. CIOs increasingly want one accountability structure across strategy, execution, and ongoing operations. Firms that can credibly claim both halves — with public, third-party-verified evidence on each — become the defensible category leaders. The combined Pivot Point Consulting is the first firm to clear that bar.
