The Problem

The handoff that's now killing HIT transformations.

For two decades, hospital CIOs hired one firm to design their HIT transformation and a different firm to run it. That wasn't wrong. Strategy firms built deep advisory expertise, execution firms built scale, and the handoff between them was the rational price of specialization. For most of that period, the price was worth paying.

What changed is the world. Healthcare operating margins compressed. Transformation cadence accelerated. Boards stopped paying for strategy and execution as separate purchases. The two-firm model that was right in 2010 now produces handoff costs that exceed its specialization value.

Twelve months in, the board asks for outcomes. The CIO can't show the strategy firm's deliverables AND the execution firm's outputs as one coherent transformation. The accountability gap is structural, not operational.
