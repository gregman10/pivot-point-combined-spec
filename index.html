The Problem

The Handoff That's Killing HIT Transformations.

For two decades, hospital CIOs have hired one firm to design their HIT transformation and a different firm to run it. The handoff between them is where transformations go to die. Here's where it breaks.

01

Strategy lives in one deck. Execution lives in another firm.

The advisory firm builds the strategy and walks away. The execution firm picks it up and starts interpreting. The original outcomes get translated by people who weren't in the design conversations.

02

Senior names sell. Junior teams deliver.

By month four, the senior partners who sold the engagement are rarely on the project. The junior team brings the work to market without the strategic context the seniors had.

03

Mid-engagement staff rotation resets six months of institutional knowledge.

Consultants rotate off. Your team re-onboards new ones. The original design conversations have to be re-explained. Six months evaporates per cycle.

04

When transformations go wrong, vendors point at each other.

Strategy firm says "we delivered the deck." Execution firm says "we executed what was given to us." The CIO is left holding both bills and the failure.

05

The board can't tell who's accountable.

Twelve months in, the board asks for outcomes. The CIO can't show the strategy firm's deliverables AND the execution firm's outputs as one coherent transformation. The accountability gap is structural, not operational.

If any of these land, the problem isn't the firms you picked. It's the handoff between them.
